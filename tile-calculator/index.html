How to Use the Tile Calculator

Enter the length and width of the area you want to tile, then enter the dimensions of a single tile. Choose Metric or Imperial units to match your measurements.

Add a waste allowance to account for cuts, breakage, and spare tiles. The calculator uses 10% by default, but you can adjust this percentage to suit your project.

If you know how many tiles are included in each box, enter the number to estimate how many boxes you may need to purchase. You can also enter a price per tile or price per box to estimate the total material cost.

How Tile Calculation Works

The calculator first multiplies the length and width of your project area to determine the total surface area.

It then calculates the area of one tile and divides the project area by the tile area to estimate how many tiles are required before waste.

Your selected waste allowance is then added to the estimate, and the result is rounded up to a whole tile so that you have enough tiles to cover the calculated area.

How Much Extra Tile Should I Buy?

Extra tiles are useful because some tiles may need to be cut during installation, and others may be damaged or broken. Keeping spare tiles can also be helpful for future repairs.

The calculator uses a 10% waste allowance by default. You can adjust this percentage depending on your project, tile layout, installation pattern, and the amount of cutting required.

Projects with diagonal layouts, complex patterns, irregular spaces, or many cuts may require a higher waste allowance.

How to Calculate Tile Boxes

Tiles are often sold in boxes rather than individually. If you know how many tiles are included in each box, enter that number in the Tiles Per Box field.

The calculator divides the recommended number of tiles by the number of tiles in each box and rounds up to the next full box. This helps ensure that the purchase contains at least the calculated number of tiles required.

Tile Calculator Example

Imagine you are tiling an area that is 4 metres long and 3 metres wide. The total surface area is 12 square metres.

If each tile measures 60 cm × 60 cm, one tile covers 0.36 square metres. Dividing the surface area by the tile area gives an initial estimate of 34 tiles after rounding up.

With a 10% waste allowance, the calculator recommends 37 tiles. If the tiles are sold in boxes of four, you would need 10 boxes, giving you 40 tiles in total.

If you enter a price per tile or price per box, the calculator can also estimate the total purchase cost.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many tiles do I need?

The number of tiles you need depends on the size of the area, the size of each tile, and the waste allowance you choose. Enter these measurements into the calculator to estimate the required quantity.

How do I calculate tiles for a floor or wall?

Measure the length and width of the surface and enter those dimensions into the calculator. Then enter the length and width of one tile. The calculator compares the total surface area with the area covered by each tile.

How much tile waste should I allow?

The calculator starts with a 10% waste allowance. The appropriate amount can vary depending on the layout, tile pattern, room shape, and amount of cutting required, so you can adjust the percentage for your project.

Does the calculator work with Metric and Imperial measurements?

Yes. Select Metric to enter the project area in metres and tile dimensions in centimetres, or select Imperial to enter the project area in feet and tile dimensions in inches.

How many boxes of tiles should I buy?

Enter the number of tiles included in each box. The calculator will round the required quantity up to the next full box and show the total number of tiles that would be purchased.

Can I estimate the cost of my tiles?

Yes. Choose whether your price is per tile or per box, select your currency, and enter the price. The calculator will estimate the total purchase cost based on the selected pricing method.

Important Note

This calculator provides an estimate for planning purposes. Actual tile requirements can vary depending on installation pattern, cuts, room shape, damaged tiles, manufacturer packaging, and other project-specific factors.

Always check the tile manufacturer's specifications and consider your installer or supplier's recommendations before purchasing materials.
